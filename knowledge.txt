SECTION 4: WASTE & RECYCLING SERVICES FOR RESIDENTS - EXPANDED
================================================================================

[GENERAL WASTE, RECYCLING, AND FOGO COLLECTION]

Collection Schedule Overview:
- General waste (red or dark-green lid): Collected weekly
- Recycling (yellow lid): Collected fortnightly (except low-rise multi-unit buildings with weekly recycling)
- Food and garden organics/FOGO (lime-green lid): Collected weekly for all households and low-rise apartments

Recycling and general waste collections occur on all public holidays except Good Friday and Christmas Day (collections on following day, usually Saturday).

Collection Zones:
City of Melbourne serves five collection zones covering different suburbs:

Zone 1: North Melbourne and Parkville - Monday collection
Zone 2: South Yarra, Southbank, Melbourne, South Wharf, Docklands, Port Melbourne, Kensington - Tuesday collection
Zone 3: Carlton North, Carlton, North Melbourne, West Melbourne - Wednesday collection
Zone 4: Flemington, Kensington, North Melbourne, West Melbourne - Thursday collection
Zone 5: Carlton, East Melbourne, Melbourne - Friday collection

To find your collection zone:
1. Check the waste collection calendar for your area (Area A or B)
2. Use the online collection days map at www.melbourne.vic.gov.au
3. Contact Council on +61 3 9658 9658

Placement Rules:
- Place bins out for collection between 6pm the night before and 6am on collection day
- Ensure bin lid is fully closed with no waste on top or beside it
- Place bins 30cm apart and at least 1 metre from power poles, trees, and street signs
- Bins must be placed on the street verge, not on private property
- Residents must return bins to property after collection
- Apartment building managers may oversee bin collection
- Do not overfill bins - lid must close properly

Bin Sizes:
- General waste: 120-litre standard (some older dark-green lids, progressively being replaced with red lids)
- Recycling: 120-litre or 240-litre standard (can request free upgrade)
- FOGO: Varies by property type (check with Council)

Note: Some general waste bins still have older dark-green lids - these are being progressively updated to the industry standard red lids.

[WHAT GOES IN EACH BIN - DETAILED GUIDE]

RECYCLING (YELLOW LID) - CAN ACCEPT:
- Hard/rigid plastic bottles and containers (milk bottles, soft drink bottles, detergent bottles, shampoo bottles)
- Paper and cardboard including clean pizza boxes (no waxed or greasy cardboard)
- Aluminium cans, foil (scrunch into a ball), and trays
- Steel cans
- Empty aerosol cans
- Glass bottles and jars (that contained food or drink)
- Cardboard boxes flattened (no larger than 120L bin when broken down)
- Newspaper and magazines
- Phone books and directories
- Envelope windows
- Cartons and juice containers (clean only)

Recycling Placement: Put items loose into bin. Never put in plastic bags or tie up. Ensure lid is closed to prevent litter. Contamination (wrong items) can result in entire bin not being collected.

RECYCLING (YELLOW LID) - CANNOT ACCEPT:
- Soft plastics (food wrappers, plastic bags, chip packets)
- Cling wrap
- Bagged garbage or bagged recycling
- Long-life milk or juice cartons
- Nappies or nappy wipes
- Expanded polystyrene and foam (including meat trays)
- Window glass, glassware, mirrors, Pyrex, crockery
- Food or garden waste
- Textiles and clothing
- Batteries and electronic waste
- Syringes
- Waxed cardboard
- Ceramics and porcelain
- Plastic bags (go in red bin)
- Shredded paper
- Plastic-coated paper (takeaway containers)

Alternative disposal for these items available through A-Z Recycling and Waste Disposal Guide.

GENERAL WASTE (RED LID) - CAN ACCEPT:
- General rubbish
- Nappies (wrapped or bagged)
- Food waste only if no FOGO service available
- Polystyrene and foam (including meat trays)
- Plastic bags and soft plastics
- Window glass, glassware, mirrors, Pyrex, crockery (wrap carefully for safety)
- Food-soiled paper and packaging (paper plates, takeaway containers)
- Non-recyclable packaging
- Incontinence products
- Broken ceramics (wrapped for safety)
- Tissues and paper towels

GENERAL WASTE (RED LID) - CANNOT ACCEPT:
- Items that can be recycled in yellow lid bin
- Garden waste (use FOGO bin or book free monthly garden waste collection)
- Commercial quantities of food waste
- Building material including bricks, concrete, timber
- Liquid waste or ashes
- Chemicals, paints, oils, solvents
- Syringes
- Car parts or motor oil
- Batteries or other electronic waste
- Unwrapped vacuum dust and polystyrene beads
- Asbestos-containing materials
- Medical waste
- Large furniture (use hard waste collection)

For these items, use alternative services: free garden waste collection, hard waste collection, or drop at Dynon Road Waste and Recycling Centre.

FOOD AND GARDEN ORGANICS (LIME-GREEN LID) - EXPANDED GUIDE:
Weekly FOGO collection is provided to all households and low-rise apartments.

Food Scraps Accepted:
- Fruit and vegetable scraps (peels, cores, seeds, pits)
- Meat, poultry, fish, bones
- Dairy products (cheese, butter, milk, yogurt)
- Cooked and uncooked food
- Bread, rice, pasta, cereals
- Tea bags and coffee grounds
- Nuts and seeds
- Oils and fats
- Takeaway food scraps
- Eggs and eggshells
- Pasta sauce and sauces
- Gravy and condiments
- Pet food (non-meat)

Garden Waste Accepted:
- Grass clippings
- Leaves and garden trimmings
- Branches (cut to fit in bin)
- Flowers and plants
- Garden soil and potting mix
- Hay and straw
- Weeds
- Bark and mulch
- Twigs and small branches

Food-Soiled Paper:
- Used paper towels
- Paper bags contaminated with food
- Paper napkins and serviettes
- Compostable paper plates and cutlery
- Paper takeaway boxes (food-soiled)

What NOT to include in FOGO:
- Glass, metal, plastic
- Meat bones larger than size of hand (very large bones break machinery)
- Pet waste or litter
- Diseased plants
- Treated wood or garden waste with chemicals

Caddy Liners: No longer home-delivered (changed from previous system). Instead:
- Caddy liners can be collected free from service centres: Melbourne Town Hall, City Library, East Melbourne Library, Kensington Town Hall, Library at the Dock, North Melbourne Library, Southbank Library, narrm ngarrgu Library, Kathleen Syme Library
- Must bring proof of residency (driver licence, utility bill, lease agreement)
- Limited to one pack per customer per visit
- Alternative: Use compostable liners (paper bag, newspaper, or paper towel)
- No liner needed - items can go directly into bin
- Commercial compostable liners must show certified compostable symbol and code AS4736 or AS5810
- Liners must be certified compostable under Australian standards
- Store caddy liners in freezer to prevent odours

[OTHER WASTE & RECYCLING SERVICES - DETAILED]

Free Services Available to Residents:

- Booking process: Online through Council website

Paid Services:

- Location: Dynon Road, Melbourne

- Fees: Some services free; fees apply for certain materials
- Contact: +61 3 9658 9658

If there is a problem with your bin or collection was missed:
- Report online through Council website www.melbourne.vic.gov.au

Common Issues:

- Bin overflowing: Pack items properly, use hard waste collection if needed
- Locked bins: Ensure accessible on collection day
- Damaged bins: Council replaces free of charge

[RESIDENTIAL PARKING PERMITS - DETAILED]

Purpose: Allows residents to park on street outside their home in controlled parking zones

Eligibility:
- Must be resident of City of Melbourne
- Must have off-street parking area on property (can't park there due to blockage or insufficient space)
- Must be unable to use off-street parking
- Property must be in controlled parking zone

Application Process:
1. Apply online through Council website or in person at Melbourne Town Hall
2. Provide proof of residency (driver's licence, utility bill, lease agreement)
3. Provide vehicle registration details and full registration number
4. Pay annual permit fee ($250-400 depending on location and vehicle type)
5. Permit issued - valid for 12 months from issue date
6. Permit displayed in vehicle (windscreen visible)

Permit Types:
- Resident permit: For vehicles permanently based at property (resident's name)
- Visitor permit: Short-term parking for guests (booklet of daily permits)
- Commercial vehicle permit: For small commercial vehicles based at property
- Motorcycle permit: Reduced fee for motorcycles ($100-150)
- Additional vehicle permit: Second permit for household ($400+)

Resident Parking Zones:
City of Melbourne has multiple residential parking zones covering:
- Carlton (Zone C)
- East Melbourne (Zone E)
- Kensington (Zone K)
- North Melbourne (Zone N)
- Parkville (Zone P)
- West Melbourne (Zone W)
- Parts of Southbank and Docklands (Zones S, D)

Zone hours vary:
- Typical hours: 7am-11pm (some zones extended)
- Some zones: 24/7 control
- Check specific zone signage for exact hours

Permit Renewal:
- Renew online 30 days before expiry
- Can renew in person at Town Hall
- Annual renewal required
- Early renewal available (up to 60 days before expiry)
- Auto-renewal option available (payment via card on file)

[ON-STREET PAID PARKING - DETAILED]

Parking Meters:
- Located throughout CBD and activity centres
- Time limits vary: 30 min to 4 hours depending on location
- Operating hours: Typically 7am-11pm (some 24/7)
- Rates: $1.00-$4.50 per hour depending on location and demand
- Payment methods: Phone, coins, card, SMS

Payment Methods:
- Phone via ParkMelbourne app (recommended)
- Coins in meter (where available)
- Card payment at meters (EFTPOS enabled)
- SMS parking (in designated areas)
- Online payment through Council website

Parking Infringements (Fines) - DETAILED PROCESS:
- Parking fine amount: $75-$200 depending on offence (expired meter, time limit exceeded, no permit)
- Payment deadline: 28 days from issue
- Pay online (after 3 days), by phone, post office, or mail
- Late payment penalty: Additional $16-32 if paid after 28 days
- Monthly direct debit option available (for extended payment)
- Interest accrues at 10% p.a. on overdue amounts

Review/Appeal Process:
- Can request review within 28 days
- Lodge appeal online or in person at Town Hall
- Council reviews circumstance and evidence provided
- Response typically within 28 days
- Further appeal to Fines Victoria available if still contested
- Common grounds for appeal: Damaged signage, exceptional circumstances

Hardship Options:
- Extension of payment deadline (7-28 days possible)
- Reduced instalment plan available
- Contact Council for assessment if experiencing financial hardship
- Financial counselling referral available
- Information about hardship assistance programs

[PAID PARKING AREAS - ZONES & RATES - DETAILED]

CBD Parking Areas:
- Swanston Street and surrounding (primary CBD): $3.00-$4.50/hour

SECTION 6: PLANNING & BUILDING PERMITS - GREATLY EXPANDED

[PLANNING PERMITS & BUILDING PERMITS - COMPREHENSIVE OVERVIEW]

Two types of approvals typically required:
1. Planning Permit: Required for land use, site layout, setback, heritage, parking, external appearance
2. Building Permit: Required for structural work, safety standards, disability access, energy efficiency

Both required for most significant works. Some works require only one permit.

Application Process:
1. Determine what approvals are needed (use Greenlight portal or contact Council)
2. Prepare documentation and plans (or hire consultant)
3. Submit application online via Greenlight or in person
4. Council assesses against Planning Scheme
5. May request further information (RFI) within assessment period
6. Council makes decision (approval or refusal)
7. Obtain Building Permit after Planning approval if required
8. Carry out works in compliance with permits
9. Council conducts inspections during construction

[PLANNING SCHEME ZONES & OVERLAYS - DETAILED]

City of Melbourne Planning Scheme divides municipality into zones:

Zones:
- CBD Zone: CBD business district (Swanston to Spring Street, north to Victoria Street)
- Mixed Use Zone: Mixed residential and commercial (inner areas)
- Residential Zone: Mainly residential (Parkville, Kensington)
- Commercial 1 Zone: Neighbourhood shopping centres (Carlisle Street, Swan Street areas)
- Commercial 2 Zone: Office and business precincts (Southbank precinct)
- Industrial 1 Zone: Light industrial (limited areas)
- Parks and Recreation Zone: Public open space (parks, gardens)
- Public Facilities Zone: Government buildings, schools, universities

Zone Purposes:
- CBD Zone: Business, retail, entertainment, cultural, hospitality
- Mixed Use: Residential with ground floor retail/commercial
- Residential: Single and multi-unit housing
- Commercial: Retail, services, office

Heritage Overlays - CRITICAL FOR MELBOURNE:
Many properties in City of Melbourne are heritage-listed (whole municipality historically significant):
- Heritage building: Listed building cannot be demolished without approval
- Substantial alteration: Changes to external appearance require planning permit
- Additions: Must be sympathetic to heritage character
- Internal works: Some interior alterations require approval
- Heritage Assessment: Specialist assessment may be required
- Building facades: Protection of character-defining elements

Heritage Precincts:
- Carlton: Victorian residential heritage precinct (high significance)
- East Melbourne: Victorian residential heritage precinct (extensive listing)
- Parts of North Melbourne: Heritage areas (pockets of significance)
- Inner city areas: Multiple heritage overlays throughout

Examples of Heritage Controls:
- Cannot remove or alter distinctive chimneys
- Cannot change roof pitch or materials
- Windows and doors must match original style
- Fences must be appropriate to period
- Colours must be historically appropriate
- Cannot demolish building without approval
- Additions must be subordinate to original building

Vegetation Protection Overlay:
- Protects significant trees (>10m height/>3m canopy spread)
- Cannot remove protected tree without permit
- Significant trees include elm, oak, plane trees, historic trees
- Replacement planting required if tree removed
- Damage to protected tree results in fine
- Stump removal requires approval
- Arborist assessment may be required

Flooding & Inundation Overlays:
- Some properties subject to flooding risk (particularly Southbank, Docklands, Yarra River areas)
- May affect building on ground level
- Specific building and site requirements
- Flood-resistant construction required
- Building must be elevated above flood level
- Council assessment required

[PLANNING PERMIT APPLICATIONS - EXTENSIVE DETAIL]

Applications Requiring Permits (Examples):
- Residential: Additions, extensions, deck/pergola, pool, building secondary dwelling, change of use, external colour change
- Commercial: Internal renovations affecting retail frontage, additional floorspace, change of use, new signage
- Demolition: Removing buildings or structures
- Signage: New business signs, shop signage (size >2m2)
- Car parking: Adding parking spaces
- Amenity impacts: Works affecting neighbours' light, view, privacy
- Tree removal: Any significant tree removal

Applications NOT Requiring Permits (Exempt):
- Single-storey additions under 10m2 (residential)
- Internal alterations (non-structural)
- Garden fencing under 1.8m
- Painting (non-heritage areas)
- Satellite dishes and aerials

Application Requirements (Standard):
- Site plans showing property boundaries, buildings, trees, existing structures
- Floor plans and elevation drawings to scale
- External materials and finishes (colours, textures, samples)
- Scale drawings with dimensions (typically 1:100)
- Statement of Use and Development (purpose of works)
- Heritage assessment (if in heritage area) - required by heritage consultant
- Arborist report (if affecting significant trees) - detailed tree condition assessment
- Traffic/parking impact assessment (if required for commercial/mixed-use)
- Environmental assessment (if required)
- Disability access design statement (if affecting public access)
- Engineering plans (if structural changes involved)

Request for Further Information (RFI) - DETAILED:
- Council may request additional information
- Applicant has 10 business days to respond
- Extension possible if more time needed (request in writing)
- Failure to respond may result in refusal
- Common RFI items: 
  - Additional plans/details
  - Calculations and specifications
  - Heritage assessment clarifications
  - Expert reports (arborist, engineer)
  - Traffic impact study
  - Neighbour notification details

Assessment Period:
- Standard applications: 60 calendar days
- Amended documentation: 30 days (new clock)
- Complex or controversial: Up to 120+ days
- Time counted from validation of application (not submission date)
- RFI pauses the clock

[BUILDING PERMITS - EXTENSIVE DETAIL]

Building Code Compliance:
- All building work must comply with National Construction Code (NCC)
- Structural adequacy (load-bearing, foundations)
- Fire safety (exit routes, fire resistance)
- Moisture and condensation management
- Energy efficiency (insulation, windows, heating/cooling, solar)
- Accessible and safe design (wheelchair access, doorways, stairs)
- Services and facilities (plumbing, electrical, ventilation)
- Hazardous materials (asbestos removal if required)

Application Requirements:
- Building plans and specifications (architectural design)
- Structural engineering plans (if required - typically for extensions, multi-level)
- Disability access design (DDA compliance - ramp, accessible toilet if public use)
- Energy efficiency calculations and compliance documentation
- Fire safety design certification (if required)
- Soils report (if building on poor ground or new building)
- Flood impact assessment (if in flood-prone area)
- Asbestos register (if existing building being altered)

Building Inspector Assessment Process:
- Initial assessment of documentation (compliance check)
- Site inspection(s) during construction (progress and compliance)
- Specific inspections: Foundation, framing, plumbing, electrical, fire safety
- Final inspection upon completion (entire building)
- Certificate of Completion issued if compliant (essential document)
- Defects register if issues found

[HERITAGE AREA APPLICATIONS - COMPREHENSIVE]

Heritage Building Assessment:
- If external changes proposed, heritage assessment required
- Conducted by heritage consultant or architect (Council-approved practitioners)
- Statement of Significance provided (analysis of heritage values)
- Assessment compares proposed changes to heritage values
- Council reviews heritage assessment against Planning Scheme

External Alterations Approved For Heritage Buildings:
- Window repairs in original style (matching original frames)
- Painting in historically appropriate colours (heritage colour schemes)
- Roof repairs matching original materials (same slate, tiles, timber)
- Fence repairs/replacement in style with heritage character
- Addition setback/designed to minimize visual impact on street frontage
- Replacement of damaged original elements

External Alterations Requiring Careful Assessment:
- Colour changes to significant facades (may require permit if in heritage area)
- Replacement doors, windows, glazing (must match original style/proportions)
- Additions to heritage building (must be subordinate, setback)
- Demolition of original outbuildings
- Façade modifications or cleaning
- Balcony removal or alteration

Likely to Be Refused:
- Removal of distinctive architectural features (cornices, parapets)
- Colour schemes clashing with heritage character
- Modern materials incompatible with period (aluminium windows on Victorian)
- Additions blocking significant building views from street
- Unsympathetic alterations to heritage precinct appearance
- Wholesale façade replacement

[COUNCIL RATES - COMPREHENSIVE OVERVIEW]

What are rates:
- Annual tax levied by City of Melbourne on all properties
- Funds Council services: waste, libraries, recreation, roads, planning, emergency services, community programs
- Each property assigned annual rate amount based on valuation
- Compulsory payment for all property owners
- Overdue payments attract penalties and legal action possible

[RATES ASSESSMENT & VALUATION - DETAILED]

Annual Valuation:
- Each property valued annually
- Valuation based on land value (not improvements)
- Used to calculate rate amount
- Valuation data provided by Valuation Office Victoria
- You can view your valuation online via eRates
- Can request revaluation if believe valuation incorrect

Rate Calculation Process:
- Fixed charge: $150-300 per property (standard across all properties)
- Variable charge: Percentage of land value (typically 0.25-0.35%)
- Total rate = Fixed charge + Variable charge
- Rates rebates available for eligible properties
- Pensioner concession: 50% reduction for eligible pensioners
- Charitable concession: Reduced or exempt rates for qualified organizations

Example Rate Calculation:
- Property land value: $500,000
- Fixed charge: $200
- Variable charge rate: 0.30%
- Variable charge: $500,000 x 0.30% = $1,500
- Total annual rate: $200 + $1,500 = $1,700

[RATES PAYMENT OPTIONS - DETAILED]

Payment Methods:

   - Pay full amount or arrange instalments

   - Instant payment confirmation

   - Reference: Your property address or rate number
   - Check eRates for specific BPAY reference
   - Some banks charge BPAY fees
   
3. Direct Debit: Monthly payments ($70-$150 average monthly)
   - Set up through Council or bank
   - Automatic payment on set date each month
   - 10 instalments from August to May
   - Most convenient for cash flow management
   - Requires bank account details
   
4. Phone: +61 3 9658 9658
   - Credit card or arrange payment plan
   - Monday-Friday 7:30am-6pm
   - TTY 133677 for Deaf/hard of hearing
   
5. In Person: Melbourne Town Hall
   - 120 Swanston Street, Melbourne
   - Cash, card, or cheque payment
   - Monday-Friday 8:30am-5pm
   - Queuing may be required
   
6. Mail: Cheque payment to:
   - City of Melbourne, GPO Box 1603, Melbourne VIC 3001
   - Allow processing time (2-3 weeks)

Installment Payment (4 installments):

Overdue Payments:
- Penalty: 10% of amount owing
- Applied from due date
- Interest compounds monthly
- Late notice sent 30 days after due date

Dog Registration:

Cat Registration:

- Lifetime registration available: $300-400
- Multiple cats: Each cat requires separate registration

Where to Register:
- Online through Council website
- In person at Melbourne Town Hall
- By mail to City of Melbourne, GPO Box 1603

Requirements:
- Proof of ownership (purchase receipt, microchip certificate, vet letter)
- Microchip number (mandatory for dogs and cats)
- Vaccination certificate (current rabies and distemper)
- Property address where animal will be kept
- Identification of animal (colour, breed, distinctive marks, size)

[MICROCHIPPING - DETAILED]

Mandatory Requirements:
- Dogs: Must be microchipped (before or at registration)
- Cats: Must be microchipped (before or at registration)
- Microchip number recorded on registration
- Microchip is permanent identification (implanted subcutaneously)

Benefits:
- Animal identified if lost
- Owner located through microchip database
- Animal returned if found
- National register helps trace owners
- Essential for identification in legal cases

Microchip Registration:
- Chip registered with national database
- Information includes: owner name, address, phone number
- Accessible to shelters and vets if pet found
- Update details if move house
- Register multiple contacts if preferred

Cost:
- Microchipping performed by vet: $50-100
- Can be done at registration or before
- Many vets offer discounted rates
- Essential investment for pet safety

[ANIMAL MANAGEMENT RESTRICTIONS - DETAILED]

Dog Restrictions:
- Cannot leave dog unattended in hot vehicle (danger of heat stroke, death)
- Cannot leave dog unattended outside business without shelter/water
- Cannot allow dog to bark excessively causing nuisance (complaint-driven)
- Must pick up dog waste in public places (littering offense)
- Dogs must be under control (on-lead) in public spaces unless designated off-lead area
- Cannot allow dog to injure or attack other animals or people

Aggressive Dogs:
- Dog declared dangerous if attacks person/other animal
- Owner must: notify Council, obey control order, pay fees
- Control order requirements: muzzle in public, enclosed property, compulsory insurance
- Breach of order results in infringement fine ($1,000+)

Off-Lead Areas:
- Multiple off-lead dog parks throughout municipality
- Designated times (usually daylight hours, 7am-sunset typical)
- Rules: Aggressive dogs cannot attend, owners must supervise, waste removal required
- Examples: Fitzroy Gardens Dog Exercise Area, Lincoln Reserve, Union Reserve
- Dogs must be under voice command and not aggressive

Cat Restrictions:
- Cannot allow cat to roam (curfew 7pm-7am recommended)
- Cannot allow cat to damage property or injure animals
- Cannot allow cat to cause nuisance through noise or defecation on neighbour's property

[DANGEROUS ANIMALS & DECLARED DANGEROUS DOGS - DETAILED]

Declared Dangerous Dogs:
- Dogs declared dangerous after attacking person or animal
- Owner receives declaration order
- Requirements: Control order (muzzle, contained property, insurance)
- Noncompliance results in infringement fine
- Dog can be seized if order breached
- Ongoing costs: Registration, insurance, compliance

Prohibited Breeds:
- Certain breeds listed as dangerous under legislation
- Controlled Dangerous Dogs: Pit Bull, Bull Terrier, others
- Ownership restricted or prohibited
- Sterilization required if permitted
- Council approval required for ownership
- Muzzling required in public spaces

[WILDLIFE & NATIVE ANIMALS - COMPREHENSIVE]

Native Animal Complaints:
- Contact Council if native animal causing damage/concern
- Cannot harm native animals (protected by law under Flora and Fauna Guarantee Act)
- Council may assist with relocation or exclusion
- Examples: Bats, possums, bird strikes

Wildlife Assistance:
- Injured native animals: Contact wildlife rescue service
- Bat exclusion: Professional exclusion services available (prevent entry, allow exit)
- Possum relocation: Licensed relocation service (humane removal)
- Nesting birds: Wait until nesting finished before works (protected during nesting)
- Snake sightings: Contact Council for location information

SECTION 11: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS - 50+ COMPREHENSIVE Q&A PAIRS
================================================================================

[GENERAL COUNCIL SERVICES]

Q1: How do I contact City of Melbourne Council?
A: Call +61 3 9658 9658 (Mon-Fri 7:30am-6pm), visit www.melbourne.vic.gov.au, visit Melbourne Town Hall (120 Swanston Street, 8:30am-5pm weekdays), or use the TTY relay service (133 677) if Deaf/hard of hearing.

Q2: What areas does City of Melbourne cover?
A: Melbourne CBD, Southbank, South Wharf, Docklands, West Melbourne, North Melbourne, Carlton, Parkville, East Melbourne, Kensington, and Flemington.

Q3: How do I pay my council rates?
A: Online through eRates portal (www.erates.melbourne.vic.gov.au), BPAY through your bank, direct debit (monthly), by phone, in person at Town Hall, or by mail. Payment due 15 Feb (lump sum) or instalments: 15 Sept, 15 Nov, 15 Feb, 15 May.

Q4: Can I request a payment extension for rates?
A: Yes. Contact Council on +61 3 9658 9658 to discuss hardship. Extensions may be granted if experiencing financial difficulty. Provide documentation of your circumstances.

Q5: How do I register for eRates online?
A: Go to www.erates.melbourne.vic.gov.au, click Register, enter your email or create password, enter property details, verify your email address. You'll then access your rates account online.

Q6: What's the penalty for late rates payment?
A: 10% penalty of the amount owing is applied from the due date, plus monthly interest. A late notice is sent 30 days after the due date.

Q7: Who is eligible for pensioner rates concession?
A: Pensioners receiving an eligible Centrelink pension. You receive 50% reduction in rates. Apply through Council with proof of age and pension status.

Q8: Can I pay rates by phone without internet access?
A: Yes. Call +61 3 9658 9658 (Mon-Fri 7:30am-6pm) with your rate notice number to pay by credit card or arrange instalments.

Q9: Is there a TTY service for hearing impaired residents?
A: Yes. Call TTY 133 677, then ask for +61 3 9658 9658. National relay service allows TTY users to contact Council for any service.

Q10: How do I update my contact details with Council?
A: Update online via eRates portal, call +61 3 9658 9658, or visit Melbourne Town Hall. Updating address is important for rate notices and service delivery.

[WASTE & RECYCLING]

Q11: How do I find out my bin collection day?
A: Check the waste collection calendar for your area (available online or printed copies at Melbourne Town Hall), use the online collection days map at www.melbourne.vic.gov.au, or call +61 3 9658 9658.

Q12: What should I do if my bin was not collected?
A: Check if a sticker was placed on the bin (indicating inappropriate material), wait until after 4pm on your collection day before reporting, and then report online through Council website or call +61 3 9658 9658. If your bin is missing, stolen, or damaged, it will be replaced free of charge.

Q13: Can I put plastic bags in my recycling bin?
A: No. Plastic bags jam the sorting machinery. Put items loose in the yellow bin. Soft plastics like plastic bags should go in your general waste (red) bin.

Q14: What goes in FOGO (food and garden organics)?
A: Food scraps (fruit, vegetables, meat, dairy), garden waste (grass, leaves, branches), and food-soiled paper. Do not put it in plastic bags - use compostable liners or paper bags, or no liner at all.

Q15: Where do I get caddy liners for FOGO?
A: Free caddy liners are available at Council service centres: Melbourne Town Hall, City Library, East Melbourne Library, Kensington Town Hall, Library at the Dock, North Melbourne Library, Southbank Library. Bring proof of residency. Limited to one pack per customer.

Q16: Can I book a hard waste collection?
A: Yes. One free hard waste collection per financial year. Book online through www.melbourne.vic.gov.au or phone +61 3 9658 9658. Place items on street verge before 6am on collection day.

Q17: What if I have more than one hard waste collection needed?
A: Council provides one free hard waste collection per financial year (1 July - 30 June). For additional items, visit Dynon Road Waste and Recycling Centre or use private rubbish removal service.

Q18: How often can I book free garden waste collection?
A: One free garden waste collection per month. Book online and Council will collect at scheduled time. Alternative: Use FOGO bin for smaller quantities.

Q19: What goes in general waste (red lid) bin?
A: General rubbish, nappies (wrapped), plastic bags, soft plastics, polystyrene, foam, window glass/glassware, crockery (wrapped). Do not put recyclables, garden waste, or hazardous items in general waste.

Q20: Where can I take hazardous waste?
A: Dynon Road Waste and Recycling Centre accepts chemicals, oils, paints, batteries, e-waste. Check www.melbourne.vic.gov.au for address and hours. Some items may have disposal fees.

Q21: When did Cleanaway take over waste collection?
A: From 1 July 2025, Cleanaway took over as the residential waste collector from previous provider. No changes to collection days or service - it's the same collection schedule.

Q22: Can I recycle soft plastics in yellow bin?
A: No. Soft plastics (food wrappers, chip packets) cannot go in recycling. Take to supermarkets or shopping centres that have soft plastic collection programs.

Q23: How do I report illegal dumping?
A: Report to Council on +61 3 9658 9658 or via EPA Victoria (1300 372 842). Provide location, type of waste, and timing if possible. Photos helpful.

Q24: Can I put expanded polystyrene in recycling?
A: No. Polystyrene and foam cannot be recycled in yellow bin. Must go in red bin or special disposal. Check A-Z guide for options.

Q25: What happens if my bin is overflowing?
A: Pack items carefully to fit in bin properly. If bin is genuinely overflowing due to quantity, book a hard waste collection or use extra waste services. Overfilled bins may not be collected.

[PARKING & PERMITS]

Q26: How do I apply for a residential parking permit?
A: Apply online through www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/parking or in person at Melbourne Town Hall. Provide proof of residency (driver's licence, utility bill, lease), vehicle registration, and pay annual permit fee ($250-400). Permits valid 12 months from issue.

Q27: How much does a residential parking permit cost?
A: $250-400 per year depending on location (zone) and vehicle type. Concession rates may be available for pensioners or people with disability.

Q28: What if I get a parking fine?
A: Pay within 28 days (penalty amount varies: $75-200 depending on offence). Late payment adds $16-32. You can request a review within 28 days if you believe the fine was issued incorrectly. You can pay online (after 3 days), by phone, post office, or mail.

Q29: Can I dispute a parking fine?
A: Yes. Request review within 28 days of the fine date. Provide evidence (photos of damaged signs, permit proof, etc.). Council reviews your submission and responds within 28 days. You can appeal further to Fines Victoria if still contested.

Q30: What are the parking zone hours?
A: Typical hours are 7am-11pm in residential permit zones (some zones are 24/7). Check signs at specific locations. CBD and activity centre parking may be different - check signage.

Q31: Where are the off-lead dog parks?
A: Multiple locations including Fitzroy Gardens Dog Exercise Area, Lincoln Reserve, Union Reserve, and others throughout the municipality. Designated times (usually daylight). Bring your own waste bags.

Q32: What's the on-street parking rate?
A: $1.00-$4.50 per hour depending on location. CBD and high-demand areas are more expensive. Pay through ParkMelbourne app, coins in meter, card at some meters, or SMS.

Q33: How do I pay for on-street parking via phone?
A: Download ParkMelbourne app (available on iPhone/Android), enter your registration number and location, select duration, and pay by card. You'll get SMS reminders before time expires.

Q34: Can I get an extension on a parking fine?
A: Yes. Contact Council before due date to request extension. Extensions may be granted with evidence of hardship or exceptional circumstances.

Q35: What is a tow-away zone?
A: Area where vehicles can be immediately removed and impounded. Clearly signed with tow-away zone signs. Vehicle taken to Docklands impound and retrieval fee $185+ applies.

[PETS & ANIMALS]

Q36: Do I need to register my dog?
A: Yes. All dogs over 3 months must be registered. Annual fee: $140-200 or lifetime: $500-600. Dog must be microchipped. Provide proof of ownership, microchip number, and vaccination certificate.

Q37: Do I need to register my cat?
A: Yes. All cats must be registered (any age). Annual fee: $50-70 or lifetime: $300-400. Cat must be microchipped. Provide proof of ownership, microchip number, and vaccination certificate.

Q38: Is microchipping mandatory?
A: Yes. Both dogs and cats must be microchipped before or at registration. Microchip your pet through a vet ($50-100), then register the number with your pet's registration.

Q39: What happens if my dog bites someone?
A: Report to Council immediately. Council may declare the dog dangerous, requiring owner to: follow control order (muzzle in public, contained property, insurance), pay fees, and permit may be issued with conditions.

Q40: Can I leave my dog in a hot car?
A: No. It's illegal to leave a dog unattended in a hot vehicle. Can cause heat stroke and death. Other provisions: don't leave unattended outside businesses without shelter and water, don't allow excessive barking.

Q41: What if I find a dog with a microchip?
A: Contact a local vet or animal shelter with the microchip number. They can identify the owner. Alternatively, contact Council (03 9658 9658) who can help locate the owner.

Q42: Can cats roam freely at night?
A: Council recommends keeping cats indoors 7pm-7am to prevent injury, noise complaints, and protect native wildlife. Some residents may have local bylaws restricting cat roaming.

Q43: Where can I report a dangerous dog?
A: Contact Council on +61 3 9658 9658. Provide address, time, detailed description, and any injuries. Multiple reports strengthen Council enforcement action.

Q44: What pets are allowed in rental properties?
A: Not a Council matter - determined by lease agreement. Check with landlord or real estate agent. Council can help with pet registration regardless.

Q45: How much does lifetime pet registration cost?
A: Dogs: $500-600 (one-time cost). Cats: $300-400 (one-time cost). No annual renewal needed, but update if move house.

[PLANNING & BUILDING]

Q46: Do I need a planning permit to build a pergola?
A: Most pergolas require a planning permit if in certain zones or heritage areas. Check Greenlight portal or contact Council. Some small non-enclosed structures may be exempt.

Q47: What's a Heritage Overlay?
A: A planning control protecting heritage buildings and areas. If your property has a Heritage Overlay, external changes (colour, windows, additions) may require planning permit and heritage assessment.

Q48: Do I need a planning permit for an extension?
A: Most residential extensions require both planning permit and building permit. Extensions in heritage areas require heritage assessment. Some minor extensions may not require planning permit. Check with Council first.

Q49: How long does a planning application take?
A: Standard applications: 60 days. Amended documentation: 30 days. Complex applications: up to 120+ days. Time starts from application validation. Council may request further information (RFI) which pauses the clock.

Q50: What's an RFI (Request for Further Information)?
A: Council request for additional information about your application. You have 10 business days to respond. If you don't respond, application may be refused. Provide requested documents quickly to keep process moving.

SECTION 21: DETAILED PLANNING & ZONING GUIDE
================================================================================

[MELBOURNE PLANNING ZONES - COMPREHENSIVE GUIDE]

Understanding Zones:
- CBD Zone: Central business district, commercial focus
- Mixed Use Zone: Residential with ground floor commerce
- Residential Zone: Primarily housing (single/multi-unit)
- Commercial: Shopping/business precincts
- Industrial: Manufacturing, light industry
- Public Facilities: Schools, hospitals, government

Zone Requirements by Use:

CBD Zone Properties:
- Permitted: Offices, retail, restaurants, residential, hotels, entertainment
- Parking: No minimum required for residential
- Building height: Up to 300+ metres in some parts
- Street frontage: Activation of street frontage required
- Heritage: Many buildings heritage protected

Mixed Use Zone Properties:
- Permitted: Residential with ground floor retail
- Parking: 1 space per 2-3 dwellings for residential
- Building height: 15-25 storeys typical
- Ground floor: Required to be retail/commercial/office
- Setbacks: Minimum setbacks from street frontage

Residential Zone Properties:
- Permitted: Houses, townhouses, low-rise apartments
- Parking: 1-2 spaces per dwelling
- Building height: 10-15 storeys maximum
- Setbacks: Minimum setbacks required
- Garden area: Minimum garden requirement for low-rise

[OVERLAYS & SPECIAL CONTROLS]

Heritage Overlay (HO):
- Protects buildings and areas of cultural heritage significance
- Mandatory for most of Carlton, East Melbourne, North Melbourne
- External works require planning permit
- Internal alterations may require permit
- Demolition prohibited without special approval
- Additions must be sympathetic to original design

Vegetation Protection Overlay (VPO):
- Protects significant trees over 10m height
- Cannot remove without permit
- Pruning restricted to <25% of canopy
- Replacement planting required
- Significant trees include: Elm, Oak, Plane, Heritage Trees
- Costs for compliance borne by property owner

Flooding Overlay (FO):
- Properties prone to flooding
- Building must be elevated above flood level
- Ground floor not permitted for habitable use
- Special materials and design required
- Increased insurance premiums typical

Development Plan Overlay (DPO):
- Specified precincts with development plan requirements
- Examples: Southbank, Docklands, Parkville
- Master planning requirements
- Staged development often required
- Coordination with Council vision

[PERMIT APPLICATION TYPES]

Standard Permits:
- Residential extensions and additions
- Internal alterations to heritage buildings
- Tree removal/pruning
- Signage
- Secondary dwellings

Fast-Track/Delegated Permits:
- Minor applications (small additions, colour changes)
- Approval by officers without planning panel
- 40-day assessment period (faster)
- Fewer documentation requirements

Complex Permits:
- Multi-dwelling developments
- Mixed-use development
- Large commercial expansion
- Heritage area major works
- Planning Panel review (60-120+ days)
- Public notification often required

[APPLICATION TIPS & BEST PRACTICES]

Before You Apply:
1. Check Greenlight portal for zone and overlays
2. Consult VicPlan for detailed requirements
3. Get preliminary advice from Council (free)
4. Hire professional help for complex applications
5. Budget 2-4 months from application to approval

Documentation Quality:
- Use licensed professional for plans
- Provide clear scale drawings
- Include colour elevations
- Photo of existing building/site
- Detailed specifications for materials
- Plans showing site context

Common Issues:
- Inadequate plans (most common RFI trigger)
- Missing heritage assessment in heritage area
- No parking assessment for commercial
- Unclear statements of use and development
- Lack of disability access design
- Insufficient detail on external appearance

Getting Approval:
- Address all RFI items promptly
- Provide more information than requested
- Show sensitivity to heritage/context
- Demonstrate good design
- Early consultation with Council

Dealing with Refusal:
- Request written reasons for refusal
- Assess if refusal is justified under Planning Scheme
- Consider amendments to overcome refusal
- Seek professional planning advice
- VCAT appeal available within 60 days

SOURCE_URL: www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/planning

SECTION 22: COMPLETE PARKING GUIDE - ZONES & RESTRICTIONS
================================================================================

[PARKING ZONES COMPREHENSIVE]

Zone A - CBD Core (High Parking Demand):
- Location: Swanston to Spring Street, Victoria to Flinders Streets
- Time limit: 1-2 hours typical
- Operating hours: 7am-11pm (some areas 24/7)
- Rate: $3.50-$4.50/hour
- Enforcement: Strict, regular patrols
- Use: Retail, office visits, dining

Zone B - CBD Ring (Medium Demand):
- Location: Outer CBD boundary
- Time limit: 2-4 hours
- Operating hours: 7am-6pm typical
- Rate: $2.50-$3.50/hour
- Enforcement: Regular patrols
- Use: Office, shopping, business

Zone C - Activity Centre (Low-Medium Demand):
- Location: Carlton, Southbank, Docklands
- Time limit: 1-2 hours
- Operating hours: 9am-5pm
- Rate: $1.00-$2.50/hour
- Enforcement: Moderate
- Use: Shopping, services, casual visits

Zone D - Residential (Permit Required):
- Residential parking zones (Zone N, K, W, E, etc.)
- Time limit: Unrestricted for permit holders
- Operating hours: 7am-11pm (some 24/7)
- Rate: Permit fee $250-400/year
- Enforcement: Permit checking
- Use: Resident parking protection

[PARKING VIOLATION TYPES & FINES]

Expired Meter: $75-100
- Time limit exceeded on paid meter
- Most common violation in CBD
- Enforcement starts 30 seconds after time expires
- No grace period

No Permit: $150-200
- Parking in permit zone without valid permit
- Residential zones most common
- Photo evidence required for disputed fines

Time Limit Exceeded: $100-150
- Parking beyond posted time limit
- Applies in both paid and free zones
- Photos taken by ranger

Double Parking: $200+
- Parking across two spaces
- Parking partially on footpath
- Blocking traffic flow

Disabled Space Violation: $200+
- Parking in accessible space without permit
- Most serious violation
- Immediate impounding possible

Tow-Away Zone: $185+ impound fee
- Vehicle removed immediately
- Stored at Docklands impound
- Additional daily storage fees apply

[STREET PARKING STRATEGIES]

Finding Parking in CBD:
- Use ParkMelbourne app to find nearby meters
- Check parking restrictions before leaving vehicle
- Arrive off-peak (before 9am, after 6pm if possible)
- Use off-street car parks for long duration
- Consider public transport instead

Avoiding Fines:
- Set phone reminders for time limit expiry
- Use ParkMelbourne app notifications
- Read signs carefully (some zones exempt on weekends)
- Check if loading zone operating hours allow parking
- Don't exceed posted time limits

Residential Permit Benefits:
- Unlimited parking in your zone
- No daily cost
- 12-month validity (annual renewal)
- Multiple zones available
- Available to households meeting criteria

[ACCESSIBLE PARKING - COMPLETE GUIDE]

ADPP Eligibility:
- Permanent or temporary disability affecting mobility
- Medical certification required
- Assessment by healthcare provider
- Includes: Mobility impairments, chronic conditions affecting mobility
- Application to VicRoads

ADPP Benefits:
- Free parking in marked accessible spaces
- Unlimited duration (no time limits)
- Can park in other vehicle bays if spaces full (last resort)
- Recognized in all States/Territories
- Valid for 3 years

Accessible Space Standards:
- Minimum width: 3.5m (allows wheelchair space)
- Length: 5.5m (vehicle + wheelchair access)
- Surface: Firm, level, non-slip
- Signage: Wheelchair symbol, height restrictions
- Location: Close to building entrance
- Access aisle: Free of obstacles

SECTION 24: BUILDING & CONSTRUCTION COMPLIANCE
================================================================================

[NATIONAL CONSTRUCTION CODE COMPLIANCE]

Structural Systems:
- All structural elements must comply with NCC
- Load calculations required for all structural changes
- Certified structural engineer must sign off on plans
- Specific AS/NZS standards must be met
- Building inspections verify compliance during construction

Fire Safety Requirements:
- Fire exits: Minimum 2 exits from large spaces
- Exit widths: Calculated by occupancy numbers
- Fire resistance: Materials must have specific fire ratings
- Sprinkler systems: Required in some buildings
- Emergency lighting: Exit signs and paths must be lit
- Fire extinguishers: Minimum requirements by occupancy

Moisture Management:
- Effective water exclusion required
- Dampproofing: Basement and ground floor systems
- Ventilation: To prevent condensation buildup
- Insulation: Must not trap moisture
- Flashing details: Around windows, doors, penetrations
- Gutters and drainage: Must effectively divert water

Energy Efficiency:
- Minimum insulation R-values (varies by climate zone)
- Windows: Maximum U-value standards
- Hot water systems: Minimum star rating
- Lighting: Efficiency requirements for buildings
- Thermal performance: Building must meet minimum standards
- Solar panels: Often required/recommended

Accessible Design (DDA):
- Entry: No steps, accessible path to entrance
- Doors: Minimum 800mm width accessible
- Corridors: Minimum 1500mm width for wheelchairs
- Lifts: Required in multi-storey buildings
- Accessible toilet: Required in public buildings
- Car parking: Accessible spaces mandatory

[BUILDING INSPECTION PROCESS]

Inspection Schedule:
- Foundation/Footing: After excavation, before concrete pour
- Frame Inspection: After structural frame, before installation of materials
- Plumbing Inspection: Before walls closed, after rough-in
- Electrical Inspection: Before walls closed, after rough-in
- Fire Safety: Before building completion
- Final Inspection: After all works complete

Building Permit Requirement:
- Must obtain before commencing work
- Council issues Building Permit (separate from Planning Permit)
- Permit valid for 4 years
- Can commence work only after permit issued
- Breach of permit can result in legal action
- Work must not exceed scope of permit

Certificate of Completion:
- Issued after final inspection (if compliant)
- Essential legal document
- Required for occupancy
- Required for property resale
- Without CoC: Building officially non-compliant
- Can take 6-12 months to rectify

Defects Rectification:
- Council issues defects notice if issues found
- Defects period: Typically 12 months from CoC
- Builder responsible for rectification
- Work must be completed within specified time
- Council re-inspects to verify rectification
