# 1.1 OFFICIAL NAME AND BRAND

The Council's legal and corporate name is Melbourne City Council. All
public-facing communication uses the brand "City of Melbourne" (CoM).
The official website is https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au.

# 1.2 ESTABLISHMENT AND HISTORY

Melbourne City Council began as a Town Council in 1842, formed under
the Act incorporating the Town of Melbourne. In December 1844 an
amending Act increased the council composition to 16 members
(aldermen and councillors). The coat of arms device was approved by
Council on 2 January 1843, adopted 9 February 1843, and formally
granted as armorial bearings by Letters Patent on 30 January 1940.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/council-mayor-and-lord-mayor-history

# 1.3 MUNICIPAL AREA

City of Melbourne covers 37.7 km² (often rounded to 37 km²).
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/city-maps

# 1.4 RESIDENT POPULATION

More than 159,000 people live in the City of Melbourne, across
102,000 residences. The daily population — including workers,
students, visitors and tourists — approaches 1 million people,
making CoM unique among Victorian councils in this respect.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/about-council

# 1.5 SUBURBS INSIDE THE CITY OF MELBOURNE

The City of Melbourne municipality includes the following suburbs:
Melbourne (the CBD / Hoddle Grid), Docklands, Southbank, East
Melbourne, West Melbourne, Kensington, North Melbourne, Parkville,
Carlton, plus parts of South Yarra and Port Melbourne. The Fishermans
Bend urban renewal precinct sits across the City of Melbourne and
City of Port Phillip boundary.

# 1.6 WARD STRUCTURE

City of Melbourne is unsubdivided — there are no wards. The nine
councillors, Lord Mayor and Deputy Lord Mayor are elected to represent
the whole municipality. This is standard for Victorian capital-city
councils.

# 1.7 TRADITIONAL OWNERS

The City of Melbourne respectfully acknowledges the Traditional Owners
of the land we govern, the Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung and Bunurong /
Boon Wurrung peoples of the Kulin nation, and pays respect to their
Elders past and present. Council acknowledges and honours the
unbroken spiritual, cultural and political connection they have
maintained to this place for more than 2,000 generations. Council
accepts the invitation in the Uluru Statement from the Heart and is
committed to walking together to build a better future.

# 1.8 GOVERNANCE MODEL — CITY OF MELBOURNE ACT 2001

Unlike most Victorian councils (where the mayor is elected internally
by councillors each year), the City of Melbourne has a directly
elected Lord Mayor and Deputy Lord Mayor who contest elections as a
joint ticket under the City of Melbourne Act 2001. The nine other
councillors are elected separately. This is a unique governance model
among Victorian local governments.

# 1.18 CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

Councillor conflicts of interest are governed under the Local
Government Act 2020. Councillors must declare any conflict of
interest. If the Chief Executive Officer receives declarations of
conflict of interest from six councillors on the same matter, the
CEO may write to the Minister seeking an exemption so that Council
can still consider the matter.

Complaints about the conduct of an individual officer should be
directed to the Council's Chief Executive Officer, preferably in
writing.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/code-of-conduct-protocol

# 2.1 M2050 VISION — 30-YEAR COMMUNITY VISION

# 2.2 COUNCIL PLAN 2025-2029 — STATUTORY 4-YEAR PLAN

The Council Plan 2025-2029 is Council's statutory 4-year plan. It
sets the roadmap to bring the M2050 Vision to life over the next four
years through six strategic priorities:

1. Governing for our future — we will be open, transparent, and
   financially responsible in our decisions.
2. Healthy, safe, clean and connected communities — we will support
   wellbeing, safety, and community connection.
3. Building a city for people — we will design our city around its
   residents, workers, students and visitors.
4. Vibrant and creative Melbourne — we will protect and grow
   Melbourne's creative and cultural life.
5. Leveraging our economic strengths — we will grow a resilient,
   diverse, and competitive economy.
6. Living sustainably — we will make sure everyone can enjoy parks,
   open spaces and healthy waterways close to home; we will help our
   community prepare for floods and heatwaves, continue our journey
   to zero emissions and support landscape regeneration through
   sustainable, Country-first practices.

# 2.7 EMISSIONS REDUCTION PLAN 2021-26

The Emissions Reduction Plan 2021-26 was endorsed on 19 October 2021.
It sets out 72 actions across 8 priority areas to guide Council
towards net zero emissions by 2040 for the municipality — a target
brought forward by 10 years. Since 1 January 2020, all Council
operations have been powered by 100% renewable energy through the
Melbourne Renewable Energy Project. Council operational emissions
reduced by 76% in 2019-20 compared with a 2011-12 baseline.

For the municipal target, commercial buildings account for around
60% of CoM emissions, and 80+ mid-tier commercial office buildings
require zero-carbon-ready retrofit each year from 2023 to stay on
track for 2040.

Source: https://mvga-prod-files.s3.ap-southeast-4.amazonaws.com/public/2024-05/emissions-reduction-plan-2021-26.pdf

# 3.1 HOW COM CALCULATES RATES

City of Melbourne calculates rates using the formula:
    Net Annual Value (NAV) × rate in the dollar = rate payable.

NAV is determined differently for residential and non-residential
properties:
- For residential properties, NAV is 5% of the property's value.
- For non-residential properties, NAV is approximately the annual
  market net rental less necessary maintenance expenses (excluding
  Council rates). NAV cannot be less than 5% of the Capital Improved
  Value (CIV).

Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/how-your-rates-are-calculated

# 3.2 UNIQUE CoM STRUCTURE — NAV AND DIFFERENTIAL RATING

City of Melbourne is the only Victorian local government that uses
BOTH Net Annual Value AND differential rating. Differential rating
means non-residential properties pay a higher rate in the dollar than
residential properties.

# 3.3 RATE IN THE DOLLAR 2025-26

- Residential rate in the dollar: 3.90 cents per dollar of NAV.
- Non-residential rate in the dollar: 4.69 cents per dollar of NAV
  (rounded to 4.70 cents on some Council pages).

Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/how-your-rates-are-calculated

# 3.4 RATE CAP 2025-26 AND HISTORY

The Victorian Government set the 2025-26 rate cap at 3 per cent. For
2025-26 City of Melbourne increased total rate revenue by 3 per cent,
as permitted under the cap.

Rate cap history:
- 2025-26: 3.0%
- 2024-25: 2.75%
- 2023-24: 3.5%
- 2022-23: 1.75%
- 2021-22: 1.5%
- 2020-21: 2.0%
- 2019-20: 2.5%
- 2018-19: 2.25%

Rate capping was introduced by the Victorian Government on 1 July
2016. The rate cap limits the increase in OVERALL total general
rates from the previous year — it does not apply to individual rate
notices, nor to waste charges, nor to the state's emergency services
and volunteers fund.

Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/how-your-rates-are-calculated

PART 4 — CORE SERVICES (PARKING, WASTE, PLANNING, PETS, PERMITS)
================================================================

# 4.1 PARKING — OVERVIEW

City of Melbourne operates approximately 23,000 kerbside parking
spaces across the 37 km² municipality, of which about 17,000 are
allocated for car parking. Around 200,000 off-street car parking
spaces sit across the municipality in commercial and private car
parks. Kerbside parking is regulated with differential time limits,
metered zones, and residential permit zones.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/parking-and-transport

# 4.2 KERBSIDE PARKING AND FEES

Kerbside parking fees vary by zone and time-of-day. The Kerbside
Parking and Fees page gives current fees and zone-by-zone detail:
https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/kerbside-parking-and-fees

# 4.3 RESIDENTIAL PARKING PERMITS

Residential Parking Permits are area-based (Area 1a and other
resident parking zones). Permit zones are shown on parking signs;
the permit must match the area. Eligibility is tied to the
resident's principal place of residence being inside the permit zone.
To apply or renew: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/residential-parking-permits

# 4.4 PARKING PERMITS — OTHER TYPES

Other parking permit categories include:
- Tradesperson permits (for short-term trade work at a CoM address).
- Visitor permits (linked to a residential address).
- Disability permits (coordinated with the state Accessible Parking
  Permit scheme).
- Event and film permits.

See https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/parking-permits

# 4.5 PARKING FINES

To view or pay a parking fine online:
https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/view-your-parking-fine

Parking fine review is available through Council's internal review

# 4.37 UTILITIES — WATER, ELECTRICITY, GAS, TELCO

Utilities are supplied by third parties, not by City of Melbourne.
Council does not issue water, electricity, gas or telco bills. For
faults and outages residents must contact the relevant distributor
directly — not Council.

WATER AND SEWERAGE
Water and sewerage across the City of Melbourne LGA (Melbourne CBD,
inner and western suburbs) is the responsibility of **Greater Western
Water**. Greater Western Water was formed on 1 July 2021 by the merger
of City West Water and Western Water, so older sources may refer to
"City West Water" — that body no longer trades under that name.
- Customer / accounts: https://www.gww.com.au
- Faults and sewerage emergencies (24/7): 13 44 99

ELECTRICITY — DISTRIBUTION
Electricity distribution (the poles, wires, meters and outage
response) for the Melbourne CBD and inner suburbs is **CitiPower**.
This is distinct from the retailer (the company that sends the bill);
residents can pick any retailer but the distributor is fixed by
address. Jemena distributes to north-west greater Melbourne and may
cover fringe parts of the LGA boundary.
- CitiPower faults and outages (24/7): 13 12 80
- CitiPower: https://www.citipower.com.au
- Jemena electricity faults (where applicable, 24/7): 13 16 26
- Compare retailers: Victorian Energy Compare at
  https://compare.energy.vic.gov.au

GAS — DISTRIBUTION
Gas distribution across inner and north-west Melbourne is **Jemena**.
Gas leaks and emergencies go to Jemena, not the retailer.
- Jemena gas leaks and faults (24/7): 1800 GAS LEAK / 1800 427 532

TELECOMMUNICATIONS
Fixed broadband is delivered over the NBN by retail service
providers. Council does not sell phone or internet services.
- NBN address check: https://www.nbnco.com.au
- Choose from any licensed retail service provider

# 4.38 PUBLIC TRANSPORT — TRAMS, TRAINS, BUSES, FREE TRAM ZONE

Public transport in Melbourne is operated under the State Government
of Victoria, coordinated by the Department of Transport and Planning
and branded to passengers as Public Transport Victoria (PTV). Council
does not run trams, trains or buses.

PTV / MYKI
All metropolitan trains, trams and buses use the myki ticketing
system. Myki cards are sold at train stations, 7-Elevens and PTV
outlets.
- PTV: https://www.ptv.vic.gov.au
- Plan a trip: https://www.ptv.vic.gov.au/journey
- myki information and top-up: https://www.ptv.vic.gov.au/tickets

FREE TRAM ZONE
Travel on trams is FREE anywhere fully inside Melbourne's Free Tram
Zone. The Free Tram Zone is bounded by:
- South: Flinders Street
- North: La Trobe Street
- East: Spring Street
- West: the Docklands area
- Plus William Street, Elizabeth Street and Victoria Street around
  Queen Victoria Market — these tram routes are also included.
Travel is free only if the entire trip stays within the zone. If a
trip crosses a boundary a valid myki must be touched on.

TRAMS
Melbourne has the largest urban tram network in the world. The
network is radial, focused on Melbourne CBD. Tram services run from
approximately 5am to midnight, earlier and later on some routes.
The free City Circle Tram (route 35) runs a loop around the CBD edge
and is part of the Free Tram Zone.

TRAINS
The metropolitan rail network operates as multiple radial lines
terminating at Flinders Street Station and Southern Cross Station
(both are in the City of Melbourne LGA). V/Line regional trains also
terminate at Southern Cross.

BUSES
SmartBus and local bus routes complement the tram and train network.
The NightBus/Night Network runs on Friday and Saturday nights.

AIRPORT
SkyBus runs between Melbourne Airport (Tullamarine) and Southern
Cross Station.
- SkyBus: https://www.skybus.com.au

              + Docklands + William/Elizabeth/Victoria St loop.

PART 6 — ENVIRONMENT, CLIMATE AND SUSTAINABILITY
================================================================

# 6.1 CLIMATE AND BIODIVERSITY EMERGENCY

In 2019, City of Melbourne declared a Climate and Biodiversity
Emergency. This was followed by a series of strategic instruments
including the Emissions Reduction Plan 2021-26, the Urban Forest
Strategy, the Nature in the City Strategy, and the 2040 net-zero
target.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/taking-bold-action-climate-change

# 6.2 NET ZERO BY 2040

Council's municipal net-zero emissions target is 2040, brought
forward by 10 years from earlier plans. Commercial buildings account
for around 60% of city emissions. To stay on track, 80+ mid-tier
commercial office buildings require zero-carbon-ready retrofit each
year from 2023.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/net-zero-buildings

# 6.3 MELBOURNE RENEWABLE ENERGY PROJECT (MREP)

Since 1 January 2020, all City of Melbourne operational electricity
has been powered by 100% renewable energy under the Melbourne
Renewable Energy Project (MREP) — a landmark group-procurement of
renewable generation.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/melbourne-renewable-energy-project

# 6.4 GREEN FACTOR TOOL

The Green Factor tool is Australia's first planning tool that
quantifies site-scale green infrastructure. It assigns scores to
vegetation, green roofs, green walls, and water-sensitive design
features, enabling developers and planners to demonstrate
green-infrastructure performance against a site benchmark.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/green-factor-tool

# 6.5 CITYSWITCH

CitySwitch is a free national net-zero program for office-based
businesses, run in partnership with other local governments. The
program supports 7 pathways and 16 actions toward net-zero
operations.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/cityswitch

# 6.6 URBAN FOREST STRATEGY — 3,000 TREES / YEAR

The Urban Forest Strategy targets 3,000 tree plantings per year to
cool the city by 4°C. Planting is scheduled seasonally, with
species-mix diversification, and is delivered across streets, parks
and open spaces.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/urban-forest-strategy

# 6.7 NATURE IN THE CITY STRATEGY

The Nature in the City Strategy guides biodiversity outcomes,
habitat connectivity, green-corridor planning, and ecological-
infrastructure investment across the municipality.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/nature-in-the-city-strategy

# 6.8 GREENLINE PROJECT

Greenline is Council's waterfront promenade project transforming the
north bank of the Birrarung (Yarra River) between Birrarung Marr and
the Bolte Bridge into a continuous public realm.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/greenline

# 6.9 SWIMMABLE BIRRARUNG

Swimmable Birrarung is a long-term initiative — partnering with
Melbourne Water, state agencies, and First Nations stakeholders — to
improve Birrarung (Yarra River) water quality to safe swimming
standards.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/swimmable-birrarung

# 6.10 PARKS AND RESERVES

City of Melbourne manages approximately 480 hectares of parkland
across the municipality, including Birrarung Marr, Treasury Gardens,
Fitzroy Gardens, Carlton Gardens (World Heritage listed), Flagstaff
Gardens, Princes Park and Royal Park. Adjacent state-managed parks
include Royal Botanic Gardens, Kings Domain and the Domain Parklands
(Parks Victoria).
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/parks-and-reserves

# 6.11 CLIMATE CHANGE ADAPTATION STRATEGY 2009 (HISTORICAL)

The Climate Change Adaptation Strategy 2009 was the first of its
kind in Australia. It undertook an in-depth risk assessment and
highlighted key priority areas for action. The Adaptation Action Plan
2010 outlined detailed steps Council would take to respond to the
risk assessment. Since 2009, over 100 adaptation actions have been
completed.

# 6.12 RESILIENT MELBOURNE (HISTORICAL)

Resilient Melbourne 2014-2020 was a metropolitan-scale resilience
strategy developed under the Rockefeller Foundation's 100 Resilient
Cities program. It covered shocks and stresses including climate,
health, economic and infrastructure risk.

# 6.13 TAKING BOLD ACTION ON CLIMATE CHANGE

The Taking Bold Action on Climate Change page consolidates all
current climate instruments: Emissions Reduction Plan 2021-26,
Climate Change Adaptation Strategy lineage, net-zero targets, and
MREP commitments.
Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/taking-bold-action-climate-change

City of Melbourne is particularly exposed to heatwave risk because

Heatwaves page with preparation and response information:

Council's Urban Forest Strategy targets 4°C cooling via the 3,000-
trees-per-year program as a long-term heatwave mitigation lever.

low-lying Southbank / Docklands areas. Melbourne Water is the state
agency responsible for flood management.

- Lifeline: 13 11 14 (24/7).
- Beyond Blue: 1300 22 4636 (24/7).
- Suicide Call Back Service: 1300 659 467.
- 13YARN: 13 92 76 (24/7 Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander crisis
  support line, run by Lifeline with Aboriginal-identified Crisis
  Supporters).
- In immediate danger, call Triple Zero (000).

# 7.16 WHEN TO CALL 000

For any immediate threat to life or property — fire, medical
emergency, crime in progress — call Triple Zero (000) before calling
Council. Council's switchboard (03 9658 9658) is for Council
services, not emergency response.

# 8.20 THE MELBOURNE ADVANTAGE: ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT STRATEGY 2030

The Melbourne Advantage: Economic Development Strategy 2030 is
Council's flagship economic roadmap, positioning Melbourne as the
Asia-Pacific's city of choice for innovation, talent and opportunity.
It is built on 5 strategic pillars:

1. A global powerhouse of innovation — leverage Melbourne's strength
   in innovation through its academic institutions and thousands of
   start-ups.
2. A magnet for the world's brightest minds and best enterprises —
   create pathways for underused talent and enhance appeal to global
   talent.
3. Alive with energy — invigorate the city's vibrancy and economic
   performance; cement its reputation as the preferred Asia-Pacific
   city to work, study, live and play.
4. Melbourne's competitiveness — sharpen cost-competitive edge and
   remove barriers for firms of all sizes.
5. Melbourne's liveability — maintain status as one of the world's
   most liveable and socially cohesive cities.

Source: https://www.melbourne.vic.gov.au/the-melbourne-advantage
